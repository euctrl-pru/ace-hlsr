3  Economic cost-effectiveness

The concept of economic cost-effectiveness, developed by the PRC, is defined as the sum of gate-to-gate ATM/CNS provision costs and en‐route and airport ground ATFM delays costs, expressed per composite flight-hour. This economic performance indicator is intended to capture trade‐offs between these two components, with delays arising from temporary imbalances between available capacity and traffic demand1.

Figure 3.1 shows preliminary results on the changes in economic cost-effectiveness over 2015 - 2025 at Pan-European system level. Unit economic costs fell by -7.1% in 2025, driven by a -18.8% decrease in the unit costs of ATFM delays, and a -2.8% reduction in ATM/CNS provision costs per composite flight-hour. Although gate-to-gate ATFM delays fell from 29.6 million minutes in 2024 to 25.2 million minutes in 2025, they remained relatively high, with estimated costs of €3.4 billion. ATFM delay costs accounted for 23% of unit economic costs, down from the record high of 27% in 2024 but close to the shares recorded in 2018, 2019 and 2023.

Figure 3.2 shows preliminary results at ANSP level (dotted lines represent the 1st and 3rd quartiles, €433 and €928, respectively).

For ten ANSPs ATFM delays represented more than 30% of their unit economic costs (see ANSPs with the largest red and yellow portions, e.g. M-NAV, BHANSA, HASP, SMATSA, DSNA, etc.). In absolute terms (cumulative ATFM delays in minutes) DSNA, ENAIRE and HASP generated 50% of the Pan-European system total ATFM delays.

The primary sources of ATFM delays in 2025 were capacity and staffing shortages (47% of total ATFM delays), followed by adverse weather conditions (34% of total ATFM delays).

Further analysis of the relationship between changes in ANSPs costs, traffic and unit costs will be presented in the forthcoming ACE report.
